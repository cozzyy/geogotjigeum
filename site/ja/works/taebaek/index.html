太白山脈(テベクサンメク)のロケ地・舞台マップ

太白山脈(テベクサンメク)の場所をすべて見る → TOP5・キャスト・全100か所を見る

解放後の混乱期から朝鮮戦争までを貫く、全4部10巻の大河小説。麗水・順天10・19事件直後の筏橋(ボルギョ)を舞台に、ヨム・サンジン、ヨム・サング兄弟をはじめとする人々が、イデオロギー・土地・家族・生存の岐路でそれぞれ違う選択をしていく姿を描く。全羅南道宝城郡筏橋邑には、物語の舞台そのものを今に伝える実… 実際のロケ地と物語の舞台、周辺の旅行情報を区別して紹介します。

『太白山脈』を読み終えると、筏橋(ボルギョ)という地名がやけに心に残る。

小説に出てくる地名が実際に存在するのか気になって調べてみたら、思っていたよりずっと多くのものが実在していた。

まず小説に出てくる召鶴橋(ソファダリ)がそうだ。正式名称は芙蓉橋で、1931年に完成した実在の橋なんだけど、今でも筏橋の住民たちはこの橋をただ「ソファダリ」と呼んでいるという。小説に出てくる巫女ソファの名前が、実際の地名に取って代わってしまったわけだ。

筏橋の虹橋(ホンギョ)はそれよりもずっと古い。朝鮮・英祖5年(1729年)に初めて架けられた石橋で、今は宝物第304号に指定されている。「筏の橋」という意味の筏橋(ボルギョ)という地名自体が、まさにこの橋に由来しているという。

ただし、小説の冒頭に出てくるヒョン富者(プジャ)の家とソファの家は、もともとあった建物ではない。2008年に太白山脈文学館が建てられた際に一緒に復元・再現された空間だということは、はっきり伝えておきたい。

순천만 국가정원, 전남 순천시 — 태백산맥 배경 인근 여행지 · Wikimedia Commons

逆に、小説第1部の終盤に出てくる解放区「ユロ」は、宝城郡に実際にユロ面という行政区域は存在するものの、小説の中の空間と正確に一致するかどうかは確定していないという。このサイトは特定の実在する村に不必要な烙印を押さないよう、この項目の座標をあえて公開していない。

この小説が扱う麗水・順天10・19事件と朝鮮戦争は、フィクションではなく実際に起きた出来事だ。1948年の事件が鎮圧された直後、左翼勢力が去った筏橋に警察と右翼勢力が戻ってくるところから物語は始まる。

ヨム・サンジンを中心とする左翼勢力が一時的にユロ一帯を掌握するものの、まもなく追われる立場になっていく序盤は、この小説が勝者と敗者を単純に分けて描いていないことを示す部分でもある。

この小説の本当の重みは、ヨム・サンジンとヨム・サング兄弟が、それぞれ異なる権力に寄りかかりながらまったく違う人生へと分かれていくところにある。同じ親のもとで育った兄弟が、時代のせいで別々の道を歩まざるを得なかった ― その先で二人を待っているものが何なのかは、ここでは明かさないでおく。ただ、どちらの道も完全な勝利としては描かれていない、ということだけは伝えておきたい。

チョン・ハソプが巫女ソファの家に身を隠すところから始まる個人の物語も印象的だ。イデオロギー・身分・信仰・生存が一人の人間の中で重なり合うこの関係は、大きな歴史の叙述の中でも一人ひとりの居場所を見失わないでおこうとするこの小説の姿勢を表している。

보성 녹차밭, 전남 보성군 — 벌교읍이 속한 보성군의 대표 명소 · Wikimedia Commons

ハ・デチのような貧しい農民たちが土地の問題をきっかけに左翼運動に加わっていく場面は、イデオロギーというものが結局どれほど具体的な生存の問題と結びついていたかを浮き彫りにする。この小説を単なる「イデオロギー小説」として読み切れない理由がここにある。

1950年に朝鮮戦争が勃発して統治権力が再び入れ替わり、同じ筏橋の住民たちが協力者・反逆者・被害者として再び分類されていく過程は、この小説の中でもっとも痛みを伴って読める部分だ。山に入った人々が寒さと飢え、討伐に耐える一方で、残された家族や村もまた監視と報復の中で生き延びていく。

1953年の休戦後もなお解決されない土地と分断の問題がこの小説の中でどう描かれているかは、ぜひ本編を読んで確かめてほしい。この物語は、勝利や和解できれいに締めくくられる類のものではない。

実際に筏橋を歩くということは、ちょっとした文学散歩以上の意味を持つように思う。太白山脈文学館から始まり、ヒョン富者の家、ソファの家、ソファダリ、筏橋の虹橋、宝城旅館まで ― この道を歩いていると、文学的想像力と実際の歴史が縦糸と横糸のように織り込まれていることを、体で感じることになる。

虹橋の上に立ったとき、たった一つの橋の名前に一つの小説の重みが乗っているのだと改めて実感したことを覚えている。筏橋に行くなら、これらの橋の名前の由来から一つずつたどりながら歩いてみることをお勧めしたい。

太白山脈(テベクサンメク)のすべての場所（100か所）

公式・直接

벌교읍 (전남 보성군 벌교읍)

The real place name that functions as the story's true protagonist. The novel opens just after the Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident of 1948 was suppressed, as leftist forces withdraw from this small town and police and rightist youth groups return. Named for the raft bridge ("beol-gyo") that once crossed the Beolgyo Stream, this town became the stage for a shifting map of power, where the same residents are repeatedly reclassified as collaborators, suspects, or victims. After the Gyeongjeon rail line opened in 1922, Beolgyo grew rapidly as a transportation hub for goods from eastern Jeollanam-do, and was elevated to town (eup) status in 1937.

公式・直接

태백산맥문학관 (전남 보성군 벌교읍 홍암로 89-19)

The largest literary-tourism museum of its kind in Korea, opened on November 21, 2008. It sits at the foot of Jeseoksan Mountain, right beside the sites recreating the Hyeon family house and Sohwa's house from the novel's opening chapters. The museum displays a portion of author Jo Jung-rae's handwritten manuscript — roughly 16,500 pages in total — along with research materials, character guides, and the historical context of the Yeosu-Suncheon Incident and the Korean War, helping first-time visitors get their bearings before diving into the ten-volume novel. As a real, operating museum, it's also the practical starting point for every other location on this map.

関連

현부자네 집 (태백산맥문학관 인근, 제석산 자락)

A recreation of the house belonging to the fictional Hyeon family, local landlords in Beolgyo who appear in the novel's opening chapters. Built in a hanok style with Japanese-influenced garden elements, it overlooks the Jungdo embankment below. While the Hyeon family itself is fictional, the house's architecture is a real, physical record of how colonial-era landlord power actually lived — the unresolved postwar land problem and the shadow of collaboration are, in a sense, compressed into this single building.

関連

소화의 집 (태백산맥문학관 경내, 제석산 자락)

A recreation of the house where the shaman Sohwa lived — the key location where she hides the fugitive Hyeop Ha-seop. The relationship between Sohwa and Ha-seop, where ideology, social status, faith, and survival all intersect, is often cited as the novel's most personal and symbolic thread. Since Sohwa is a fictional character, this house was likewise restored and recreated as part of the museum complex, not preserved in its original form.

関連

김범우의 집 (벌교읍 시내)

Depicted as the home of Kim Beom-u, the moderate intellectual who refuses to judge people by either side's slogans alone, viewing the era instead through the lens of nation, humanity, and daily life — a contrast to his own brother Kim Beom-jun, who joins the leftist cause. The house also stands for how political choices can split even within one family. Public accessibility and the exact location vary between sources, so it's worth checking the latest information before visiting.

公式・直接

소화다리 (정식명 부용교, 보성군 벌교읍 회정리)

A two-lane reinforced-concrete bridge completed in June 1931. Its official name is Buyong Bridge, but locals still universally call it "Sohwa Bridge." A real crossing over the Beolgyo Stream, it's also portrayed in the novel as a site where repeated violence and memory become entangled each time power changes hands. It was restored to its present form in 2007. This site keeps the novel's fictional events at this bridge separate from the confirmed historical fact of its 1931 construction.

公式・直接

벌교 홍교 (보물 제304호, 보성군 벌교읍 벌교리 154-1)

A stone rainbow bridge spanning the Beolgyo Stream — 27 meters long, about 3 meters high, and roughly 4.5 meters wide. Tradition holds it was first built in 1729 by two Buddhist monks, Choan and Seupseong, from Suncheon's Seonamsa Temple, repaired in 1737 and 1844, and restored to its present form over four years from 1981 to 1984. It's the largest surviving stone rainbow bridge in Korea, and locals still cross it today. The town's very name, Beolgyo ("raft bridge"), comes from the log-raft bridge that once stood on this spot.

公式・直接

보성여관 (등록문화재 제132호, 벌교초등학교 옆)

A wooden, Japanese-style inn building completed in 1935, still showing what Beolgyo's modern transportation, lodging, and commerce looked like in that era. Left abandoned for years, it was dismantled and restored by the National Trust for Cultural Heritage of Korea and reopened as a lodging and cultural-experience space on June 7, 2012. It isn't tied to a specific scene in the novel, but it's a single building that makes tangible how Beolgyo grew into a commercial town after the colonial-era Gyeongjeon rail line opened.

体験施設

옛 벌교금융조합 터 (벌교 홍교 인근)

A candidate site symbolizing the region's colonial-era financial and land structures, useful for understanding the novel's themes of landlordism and tenant farming. However, whether this building still survives in its original form, and what it's used for today, varies between sources — this site has not been able to fully confirm its current status. It's said to have stood within the old commercial district between Hongyo Bridge and Beolgyo Station.

公式・直接

벌교역 (전남 보성군 벌교읍)

A real train station that opened alongside the Gyeongjeon rail line in 1922, a turning point that let Beolgyo grow rapidly into a transportation hub and collection point for goods from eastern Jeollanam-do. In the novel, it's portrayed as a crossing point for people, information, and the movements of police and military — who passes through this station, and when, becomes a real-time indicator of the shifting political tides. Today's rail lines and service differ from the novel's era, so visitors should distinguish between the historical route and the current one.

公式・直接

벌교 철다리 (경전선 철교, 중도방죽 초입)

A real railway bridge dating to the 1930 opening of the western Gyeongjeon line, standing at the point where the Jungdo embankment begins. It's a genuine piece of Beolgyo's modern industrial infrastructure, and in the novel it's also the site where Yeom Sang-gu, brother of the leftist leader Yeom Sang-jin, fights to take control of Beolgyo's underworld — a symbolic space showing how two brothers, leaning on opposite sources of power, end up living completely different lives.

公式・直接

중도방죽 (벌교읍 남쪽 간척지)

Reclaimed farmland created during the colonial era by a Japanese figure known as "Jungdo," who dammed roughly 4km of sea from Molha to Seonsu. He promised farmland to the Korean laborers who worked on the project, but the promise was never kept — a grievance that lingered among Beolgyo's farmers for generations. The novel overlays this history of land reclamation onto the backstory of poor farmers like Ha Dae-chi, who join the leftist movement over unresolved land issues. The wide fields and the view of Beolgyo's coastal waters together still exist today.

体験施設

율어(栗於) — 좌표 비공개(원문·지도 대조 전)

소설 1부 말미, 좌익 세력이 일시적으로 장악한 해방구로 등장하는 공간. 보성군에는 실제로 율어면이라는 행정구역이 존재하지만, 소설 속 "율어"가 이 실제 행정구역과 정확히 대응하는지는 원문과 지도를 대조해 확정하지 않은 상태다. 실제 지명을 섣불리 소설 속 장소와 동일시하면 특정 지역·주민에게 불필요한 낙인이 될 수 있다는 판단에 따라, 이 사이트는 좌표를 확정해 표기하지 않는다. 대신 이 항목은 "산악 지역의 유격전과 토벌"이라는 소설적 주제를 소개하는 용도로만 남겨둔다.

体験施設

조계산 (전남 순천시·보성군 접경)

Home to two major Buddhist temples, Songgwangsa and Seonamsa, this mountain is portrayed in the novel as a site of repeated guerrilla movement, military suppression, and hunger. Today it's a popular destination for hikers and temple visitors, but this site keeps the mountain's role as a tourist destination separate from its role as a site of wartime memory. Notably, the monks traditionally credited with first building Beolgyo's Hongyo Bridge belonged to Seonamsa on this very mountain — a reminder that Jogyesan has long been tied to Beolgyo's religious and historical life.

体験施設

지리산 (경남·전남·전북 접경, 천왕봉 기준)

South Korea's largest mountain range, appearing in the novel's fourth part as the final stage of the guerrilla war in the mountains. Those who fled into the mountains endured cold, starvation, injury, and suppression campaigns — some of the novel's most harrowing scenes unfold here. Per the official definition of the Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident truth commission, the incident's related period extends until April 1, 1955, when the ban on entering Jirisan was lifted — separate from the novel's narrative timeline, which ends around the 1953 armistice, the actual historical event continued beyond it. Today it's a national park and one of Korea's premier hiking destinations.

公式・直接

순천시 (전남 순천시 시내)

A real city whose name is part of the incident's official title — the "Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident" — one of the areas where the uprising that began in Yeosu spread. Neighboring the novel's setting of Beolgyo, the events depicted in the story are difficult to understand apart from what actually happened here. This site introduces Suncheon first and foremost as the site of an officially confirmed historical event, not merely as a backdrop to the novel.

公式・直接

여수시 (전남 여수시 시내)

The real point of origin for the incident: on October 19, 1948, soldiers of the 14th Regiment stationed in Yeosu, refusing orders to help suppress the Jeju April 3 uprising, rose in revolt — the reason the event's official name is the "Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident." While not a direct setting for the novel's scenes, understanding that everything happening in Beolgyo traces back to what started here in Yeosu is essential to fully grasping the novel's timeframe of 1948–1953.

公式・直接

회정리교회 (보성군 벌교읍 회정리)

A real church included on the official Beolgyo literary-tourism route, showing how modern Protestant Christianity took root early in this small town. Apart from the novel's left-right ideological conflict, it's a real reminder that Beolgyo residents of this era had an actual, ongoing religious life.

関連

자애병원 (벌교읍, 문학기행 재현 지점)

The hospital where Dr. Jeon Myeong-hwan, in the novel, treats the poor for little to no cost, portrayed as a model of the story's idea of a 'true intellectual' — charging wealthy patients properly while accepting crops or pottery from poor ones. The hospital is also the backdrop for how helping the wounded Ahn Chang-min later forces Dr. Jeon into the Bodo League.

体験施設

부용산공원 (보성군 벌교읍)

A hillside park overlooking central Beolgyo, also known as the backdrop for the folk song "Buyongsan," beloved by locals. The song is said to have offered comfort to people on both sides of the conflict during the Yeosu-Suncheon Incident and the Korean War — often cited as evidence that some emotions ran deeper than ideology.

公式・直接

구금융조합 (벌교읍 문학기행 코스)

A real building symbolizing the region's colonial-era financial and land structures, included on the official Beolgyo literary-tourism route. Along with the separately listed 'former Beolgyo Financial Cooperative site,' it's a real site of modern economic history that helps explain the novel's landlord-tenant themes.

公式・直接

벌교초등학교 (보성군 벌교읍)

A real elementary school right beside Boseong Inn, one of the stops on the literary route. The kind of school the children of the novel's characters might plausibly have attended — a reminder that ordinary daily life in Beolgyo continued even beneath the ideological conflict.

公式・直接

진트재 (벌교읍-순천 방면 고갯길)

A real mountain pass leading from Beolgyo toward Suncheon, included near the end of the literary-tourism route. As an actual route for people, information, and sometimes flight and pursuit, it's a reminder that Beolgyo wasn't an isolated town but connected to the wider region.

体験施設

벌교시외버스터미널 (보성군 벌교읍)

A real, still-operating bus terminal often used as the start or end point of the literary walking route. Alongside Beolgyo Station, it functions as one of the town's practical gateways in and out.

体験施設

벌교 오일장 (2일·7일장, 벌교읍 장터)

A real traditional market still held every five days, presumed to have functioned as a real hub of commerce and information for Beolgyo residents even in the novel's era. The lively mix of merchants, farmers, and fishermen shows what 'daily life' — something the language of ideology struggles to capture — actually looked like.

体験施設

홍교마을 (벌교 홍교 인근 마을)

A real village that formed right beside Hongyo Bridge, home to those who lived by crossing the bridge daily. Still an active residential area today, so visitors should be mindful of noise and residents' privacy.

体験施設

제석산 (보성군 벌교읍, 문학관 뒷산)

The mountain at whose foot the Taebaeksanmaek Literature Museum, the Hyeon family house, and Sohwa's house all sit. At 563 meters, it's not especially tall, but with the novel's key sites clustered around it, it functions as the real backdrop mountain of the literary pilgrimage.

体験施設

보성군청 (전남 보성군 보성읍)

The administrative center of Boseong County, to which Beolgyo belongs. The real seat of county-level administrative power during the novel's era, and still the center of Boseong's administration today.

体験施設

보성군 율어면 (실제 행정구역)

Yulyeo-myeon is a real administrative township that exists in Boseong County. As already noted in the separate "Yureo" entry, whether the novel's fictional "liberated zone" of Yureo corresponds exactly to this real township has not been confirmed against the original text and maps. This entry exists purely to introduce the real Yulyeo-myeon township itself, separate from that fictional site — we recommend not conflating the two.

体験施設

보성군 조성면

A township adjacent to Beolgyo, geographically connected to the reclaimed land around the Jungdo embankment. A neighboring area useful for understanding Beolgyo's agricultural and land-reclamation history in a wider context.

体験施設

대원사 (보성군 문덕면, 티벳박물관 겸설)

A real temple in Mundeok-myeon, Boseong County, also known today for housing a Tibet museum. A separate center of Buddhist tradition within Boseong County distinct from Songgwangsa and Seonamsa on Jogyesan, showing how the region has long organized faith communities around multiple temples.

体験施設

존제산 (보성군 조성면·순천시 접경)

A mountain straddling the border of Boseong County and Suncheon, geographically adjacent to the areas referenced as guerrilla-war territory in the novel. Today a popular hiking destination.

体験施設

보성군 겸백면

An inland, mountainous township of Boseong County, adjacent to areas referenced as guerrilla-war backdrop terrain. Today it remains a quiet farming village.

体験施設

대한다원 보성녹차밭 (보성군 보성읍 봉산리)

Boseong County's signature real green-tea plantation. Not a direct setting of the novel, but a symbol of the region's agricultural identity — alongside Beolgyo's tidal-flat cockles and the reclaimed fields of the Jungdo embankment, it shows how this region has long depended on land and sea.

순천역 (전남 순천시 역전길)

A real train station serving as the practical gateway between Beolgyo and the outside world, still served by KTX/SRT today. Since Suncheon's name is part of the incident's official title, this station carries more weight than a simple transfer point.

公式・直接

순천 매산중학교 (전남 순천시)

A real Presbyterian missionary school, portrayed in the novel as the school Seo Min-yeong attended. One of the real channels through which modern Western religion and education took root in the region via Suncheon — a plausible backdrop for how a progressive Christian intellectual like Seo Min-yeong might have been formed.

体験施設

낙안읍성 (전남 순천시 낙안면)

One of Korea's premier preserved Joseon-era walled towns, still a living folk village with real residents today. The name of the novel's character 'Naksan-daek' (Ha-seop's mother) likely derives from this very place name, Nagan, which lies geographically close to Beolgyo.

体験施設

순천만습지·순천만국가정원 (전남 순천시)

A globally significant coastal wetland, not directly tied to the novel's era, but a nearby attraction that helps convey the natural environment Suncheon is set within.

公式・直接

선암사 (전남 순천시 승주읍, 조계산)

A real temple to which the two monks traditionally credited with first building Beolgyo's Hongyo Bridge belonged. Historic enough to be called a "taego chongnim" head temple, and still an active place of monastic practice today — connected to the temple tradition behind the novel's Monk Woljoo.

公式・直接

송광사 (전남 순천시 송광면, 조계산)

A real, major temple regarded as one of Korea's Three Jewel Temples (representing the Sangha), on Jogyesan's western slope. Along with Seonamsa, it represents Jogyesan's Buddhist tradition, and offers a glimpse into how the region's long religious history endured through the turbulent era the novel depicts.

公式・直接

여수역 (전남 여수시)

The real train station serving as the gateway to the city where the Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident began. After October 19, 1948, waves of the uprising spread out from this city to towns across eastern Jeollanam-do, including Beolgyo.

公式・直接

여수 신월동 (옛 14연대 주둔지 일대)

The real area where the 14th Regiment of the National Defense Constabulary was stationed when it refused orders to help suppress the Jeju April 3 uprising on October 19, 1948 — the revolt that gave the Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident its name. Now a residential neighborhood, but still the incident's real point of origin.

体験施設

여수항 (전남 여수시)

Yeosu's real gateway port, which also served as a practical route for troops, supplies, and refugees during the incident. Today it wears a completely different face as a center of tourism and fishing.

体験施設

오동도 (전남 여수시)

Yeosu's signature real tourist destination — not a direct setting of the novel, but a reminder of the natural beauty Yeosu possessed even before the incident.

体験施設

여자만 (보성·순천·여수 접경 갯벌 바다)

A real tidal-flat bay stretching from the waters off Beolgyo through Suncheon Bay to Yeosu, illustrating the geographic fact that the novel's three settings — Beolgyo, Suncheon, and Yeosu — are actually connected across a single body of water.

体験施設

득량만 (보성군 남쪽 바다)

A real bay wrapping around the southern coast of Boseong County, which together with the Yeoja Bay off Beolgyo formed the livelihood of local fishing communities. The name "Deukryang" ("obtaining grain") is traditionally said to derive from Admiral Yi Sun-sin securing military provisions from these waters during the Imjin War.

体験施設

광주역 (광주광역시)

The real gateway to Gwangju, the central city of the Jeolla region. As several characters' relatives are portrayed working there as civil servants or bank clerks, Gwangju represented both the symbol of urban life and, at times, a place of refuge for people from Beolgyo.

公式・直接

옛 전남도청 (현 국립아시아문화전당, 광주 동구)

The real seat of Jeollanam-do provincial administration during the novel's era. Now repurposed as the Asia Culture Center, though part of the original building is preserved, letting visitors see a tangible trace of the era's provincial administrative power.

体験施設

무등산 (광주광역시)

Gwangju's signature real mountain — not a direct setting of the novel, but alongside Jogyesan and Jirisan near Beolgyo, a reminder that the entire Jeolla region has long depended heavily on its mountainous terrain.

公式・直接

거제도 포로수용소 유적공원 (경남 거제시)

The real site of a massive Korean War POW camp, now developed as a heritage park. In the novel, Kim Beom-u is captured by US forces while moving with North Korean troops and taken here, later released as an anti-communist POW but left with a physical disability. One of the places where real history and the novel's fiction overlap most directly.

体験施設

화엄사 (전남 구례군 마산면, 지리산)

A real, major temple on the southwestern foot of Jirisan, geographically linked to the mountain guerrilla-war setting of the novel's fourth part. The Hwaeomsa area appears in numerous accounts and records as a real route traveled by both guerrillas and suppression forces.

体験施設

피아골 (전남 구례군, 지리산)

A valley in Jirisan especially often cited as a site of fierce guerrilla fighting. Local tradition ties the valley's name itself to the brutal battles fought here; today it's also famous for autumn foliage, but it's worth visiting with awareness of the name's weight.

体験施設

뱀사골 (전북 남원시, 지리산)

A valley on Jirisan's northern slope, cited as one of several stages of the mountain guerrilla war. Today it's one of the national park's signature trails, a place where natural scenery and wartime memory overlap.

体験施設

반야봉 (지리산, 전남·전북 접경)

A peak on Jirisan considered second in importance only to Cheonwangbong, repeatedly cited as a route used by guerrilla command. Its genuinely rugged terrain helps convey, through geography alone, the extreme conditions of the mountain guerrilla war the novel depicts.

体験施設

노고단 (지리산, 전남 구례군)

A signature peak in western Jirisan, whose broad plateau-like summit made it a frequently cited guerrilla stronghold. Today it's one of Jirisan National Park's premier hiking destinations, known for sunrise views and seas of cloud.

体験施設

성삼재 (지리산, 전남 구례군)

A major mountain pass serving as the main gateway up to Nogodan, one of the highest points in Jirisan accessible by vehicle. This pass is thought to have been an important route for movement and concealment during the guerrilla war era.

体験施設

중산리 (지리산, 경남 산청군)

The southern gateway village for climbing Jirisan's Cheonwangbong peak, geographically linked, along with the peak itself, to the guerrilla-war setting of the novel's fourth part.

体験施設

대원사 (경남 산청군 삼장면, 지리산)

A real temple on Jirisan's southeastern slope — a separate temple from the similarly named Daewonsa in Mundeok-myeon, Boseong, mentioned earlier. The Daewonsa valley here is also close to areas cited as guerrilla movement routes.

体験施設

천은사 (전남 구례군, 지리산)

A real temple on Jirisan's western slope, standing along with Hwaeomsa and Nogodan on one of the main approaches into Jirisan from the Gurye side.

体験施設

백무동 (지리산, 경남 함양군)

A northeastern gateway village into Jirisan, forming the approach on the opposite side from the novel's guerrilla-war setting. Today it has a cluster of lodging and restaurants for hikers.

体験施設

백아산 (전남 화순군)

A mountain separate from Jirisan, the real "Baega-san district" referenced in the novel as the base of the guerrilla leader Yi Tae-sik's formidable "Iron Unit." Located in Hwasun County, it remains a hiking destination today.

体験施設

구례군 읍내 (전남 구례군)

A real county town serving as Jirisan's southwestern gateway, along the route to temples like Hwaeomsa and Cheoneunsa.

体験施設

곡성군 (전남 곡성군)

A real county between Suncheon and Gurye, lying along a real travel route between the novel's settings. Straddling the Seomjin River, it functioned as a genuine transit corridor for the region.

体験施設

화개장터 (경남 하동군)

A historic real marketplace at the exact point where Jeollanam-do and Gyeongsangnam-do meet, showing how people on either side of the provincial border around Jirisan actually lived and traded with one another.

体験施設

함양군 (경남 함양군)

A real county on Jirisan's northeastern side, including approach routes like Baengmudong. Shows how the guerrilla war's geographic reach extended beyond Jeollanam-do into the Jirisan foothills of Gyeongsangnam-do.

体験施設

산청군 (경남 산청군)

A real county on Jirisan's southeastern side, including approach routes like Jungsanri and Daewonsa.

体験施設

실상사 (전북 남원시, 지리산)

A real, historic temple on Jirisan's northern slope, founded in the Silla era. One of several temples encircling Jirisan, showing how the mountain has long been a space where faith, refuge, and resistance overlapped.

体験施設

진주시 (경남 진주시)

A real hub city south of Jirisan, referenced as one of the geographic hinterlands of the guerrilla war the novel depicts.

体験施設

담양군 (전남 담양군)

The real county portrayed as the hometown of the character Lee Ji-suk. Behind her turn to the leftist movement as a landlord's daughter lies the fact that this region's landlord-tenant structure wasn't so different from Beolgyo's.

体験施設

고흥군 (전남 고흥군)

The real county portrayed as the hometown of the character Seo Min-yeong. The novel ties his intellectual background — a great-grandfather said to have been a Silhak scholar, and family tradition of exchanges with Jeong Yak-yong — to this region's real Silhak (Practical Learning) tradition.

公式・直接

다산초당 (전남 강진군)

The real historic site where the late-Joseon Silhak scholar Jeong Yak-yong lived in exile after being banished for Catholic persecution in 1801. Connected to the novel's premise that Seo Min-yeong's great-grandfather exchanged ideas with Jeong Yak-yong, tracing a fictional lineage of agricultural reform spirit passed down to Seo Min-yeong.

体験施設

목포시 (전남 목포시)

A real port city in western Jeollanam-do, cited among the routes people used to travel or flee during the incident's era. Where Beolgyo, Suncheon, and Yeosu represent eastern Jeollanam-do, Mokpo served as the gateway to the west.

公式・直接

옛 광주교도소 터 (광주광역시)

The former site of a facility known to have held people detained on leftist-related charges. A place that conveys the real weight of how political prisoners were treated in this era — a history of state violence that calls for a respectful approach.

公式・直接

판문점 (경기도 파주시, 공동경비구역)

The real, historic site where the Korean War Armistice Agreement was signed on July 27, 1953. The war the novel depicts paused with the signing here, but the questions of land, nationhood, and division the novel raises remained unresolved even after the armistice.

公式・直接

경무대 옛터 (현 청와대 일대, 서울 종로구)

The real historical site of the presidential residence and office under the Rhee Syngman administration. The actual seat of central political power during the novel's era, showing how events in provincial towns like Beolgyo and Suncheon ultimately connected back to decisions made here.

公式・直接

거창사건추모공원 (경남 거창군)

A memorial park commemorating a real civilian massacre carried out by South Korean troops in 1951 — the very historical tragedy the novel's character Yang Hyo-seok is portrayed as having taken part in. This site does not sensationalize the event; it introduces it with the respect due a real history of state violence.

公式・直接

인천상륙작전기념관 (인천광역시)

A memorial facility commemorating the real Incheon Landing operation of September 15, 1950. Its success reversed the tide of the war, again flipping political power and repeating, in the novel's Beolgyo, the pattern of the same residents being reclassified as collaborators, traitors, or victims.

体験施設

호놀룰루 (미국 하와이주)

The real place where the novel's character "Dorajee" is portrayed as having lived. Born to first-generation Hawaiian immigrant parents and raised witnessing racial discrimination, her backstory connects to the real history of early-20th-century Korean immigration to Hawaii.

体験施設

미얀마(옛 버마) 전선 일대

The real country and region where the novel's Kim Beom-u is portrayed as having been conscripted as a student soldier before escaping. A real front where Japanese and Allied forces fought during World War II, and the starting point of Kim's later journey through OSS training back to Joseon.

体験施設

벌교천 (보성군 벌교읍을 가로지르는 하천)

The real stream running through central Beolgyo, over which Sohwa Bridge, Hongyo Bridge, and the railway bridge all cross. The town's very name derives from a raft bridge that once crossed this stream — making it, in a sense, the geographic identity of Beolgyo itself.

体験施設

벌교여자중학교 (보성군 벌교읍)

A real girls' middle school in central Beolgyo, a real space that lets one imagine the kind of educational institution the novel's young female characters might plausibly have attended.

体験施設

보성군 노동면

An inland township of Boseong County, geographically connected through its mountainous terrain to areas referenced as guerrilla-war backdrop.

体験施設

보성군 웅치면

Another inland township of Boseong County, useful alongside Beolgyo for understanding the county's mix of mountain and coastal terrain.

体験施設

보성군 복내면

A township in northern Boseong County, located along the route toward Hwasun and Suncheon.

体験施設

보성군 문덕면 소재지

The center of Mundeok-myeon, home to Daewonsa temple, sitting along the Boseong River — an area with a long history of river-based transportation.

体験施設

순천 조곡동 원도심 (전남 순천시)

Suncheon's real old downtown, once its modern commercial and administrative center, where some of the original street layout survives today.

体験施設

순천 웃장 (순천아랫장 인근 전통시장)

A real traditional market in Suncheon, which alongside the Beolgyo five-day market has served as a real center of the local commoner economy.

体験施設

순천 승주읍 (조계산 인근 마을)

A real village on the route to Seonamsa, functioning as a practical waypoint between the Jogyesan temples and central Suncheon.

公式・直接

진남관 (국보 제304호, 전남 여수시)

A real National Treasure structure, once the headquarters of Admiral Yi Sun-sin's Left Jeolla Naval Command during the Imjin War, ranked among the largest surviving wooden buildings of the Joseon era. Shows Yeosu's much longer history as a strategic center of national defense, long before the modern-era tragedy.

体験施設

돌산도 (전남 여수시)

A real island connected to Yeosu by bridge, useful for understanding the broader geography of the city.

体験施設

경도 (전남 여수시)

A small island off Yeosu's coast, part of the maritime terrain presumed to have been connected to troop and supply movements during the incident.

体験施設

순천 송광면 소재지 (송광사 인근 마을)

A real village on the route to Songgwangsa, the center of daily life on Jogyesan's western slope.

公式・直接

여수 만성리 형제묘 (전남 여수시)

A memorial site for civilians who were actually killed during the Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident. This site does not sensationalize this location; it is introduced only in the context of respecting a real history of state violence.

公式・直接

순천 매산등 추모 공간 (전남 순천시)

A space of remembrance for civilians actually killed in Suncheon during the Yeosu-Suncheon October 19 Incident, located near Maesan Middle School. This site introduces this location, too, only with respect.

体験施設

벌교 갯벌 (꼬막양식장 일대)

The real tidal flat where Beolgyo's signature cockles actually grow, connected to Yeoja Bay. Apart from the novel's ideological conflict, the fact that this tidal flat was and remains a real source of livelihood for Beolgyo's people hasn't changed.

体験施設

목포항 (전남 목포시)

A real gateway port in western Jeollanam-do, which along with the city of Mokpo handled real transportation and logistics for the western part of the region.

体験施設

순천향교 (전남 순천시)

A real Confucian local academy from the Joseon era, showing the traditional pre-modern education system of the region. Contrasted with the modern Western-style Maesan School, it offers a glimpse into how education in this region shifted from tradition to modernity.

体験施設

뱀사골대피소 (지리산 국립공원)

A real national park shelter deep in the Baemsagol valley — today a rest stop for hikers, but a reminder that this rugged terrain was the setting of the guerrilla war the novel depicts.

体験施設

이순신광장 (전남 여수시, 진남관 인근)

A real square in front of Jinnamgwan, showing how Yeosu is a city carrying both the legacy of Admiral Yi Sun-sin and the tragedy of the Yeosu-Suncheon Incident.

公式・直接

유엔기념공원 (부산광역시)

A real memorial honoring UN forces who died in the Korean War, a reminder that the war the novel depicts was not only a Korean matter but also an international one. A place showing how the story of a small town like Beolgyo was situated within a larger, global historical context.
