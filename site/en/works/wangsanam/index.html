Inside This Scene

After The King's Warden, Yeongwol looked different to me

청령포 단종어소, 강원 영월군(단종이 유배 생활을 했던 거처) · Wikimedia Commons

The Story

That river in the movie — was it really Cheongnyeongpo? Before watching The King's Warden, Danjong was honestly just the boy-king from my history textbook to me.

But after watching it, weirdly, what stuck with me wasn't the king — it was just a seventeen-year-old. Maybe because of the scenes of him eating with Eom Heung-do, joking around, sitting blankly by the river.

The film also cleared something up for me: Danjong lost his throne in the first place because of the 1453 Gyeyu Coup. His uncle, Grand Prince Suyang (later King Sejo), purged senior officials like Kim Jong-seo and Hwangbo In and effectively seized power.

Then in 1456, former Hall of Worthies scholars including Seong Sam-mun and Bak Paeng-nyeon tried to restore Danjong to the throne, were caught, and were executed — the famous 'Six Martyred Ministers' incident. After that, Danjong was completely isolated.

So the 1457 exile to Yeongwol, where the film begins, was really the final stage of a political catastrophe that had already been unfolding for years.

The closer it got to the end, the more it got to me, thinking, 'ah, I already know how this ends for him...'

장릉(莊陵), 단종의 능, 영월 · Wikimedia Commons

So I got curious about that river that kept showing up in the film. Cheongnyeongpo in Yeongwol, where Danjong was exiled. I assumed the film was shot there too — turns out it wasn't.

Cheongnyeongpo is a protected heritage site, so large-scale filming wasn't possible there. The crew scouted around Yeongwol and built a whole village on a different riverbank instead. There wasn't even a road to the spot, so they had to build one just to film.

The set was torn down completely after filming wrapped. So Eom Heung-do's house from the movie doesn't exist anywhere to visit now.

But the real Cheongnyeongpo, where Danjong was actually confined, is still there. You still have to reach it by boat — river on three sides, a cliff behind. It looks suffocating even in photos.

Even more so after watching the film. Thinking that he looked at that same river every single day and could never cross it makes the scene of him sitting by the riverbank stick in your head all over again.

There's a small story behind that scene too. Danjong sitting alone by the river wasn't originally in the script. A staff member happened to photograph Park Ji-hun sitting by the river on a break, and when Yoo Hae-jin saw the photo, he suggested, 'why don't we try shooting this as a scene' — and it made it into the film.

영월부 관아 터(관풍헌·자규루 소재지, 사적 제534호), 영월 · Wikimedia Commons

Knowing that changed how the scene looked to me. Less like a king performing his role, more like a young person who just wanted a moment alone.

Inside Cheongnyeongpo there's also Nosandae, where Danjong is said to have looked toward Hanyang; Manghyangtap, a tower he's said to have built stone by stone while thinking of Queen Jeongsun; and Gwaneumsong, a pine tree said to have watched over him.

To spoil the ending outright: that October, Danjong was forced to drink poison and died at Gwanpungheon. With no one else willing to even claim his body, Eom Heung-do alone risked his life to secretly retrieve it and bury it.

And here's the part that genuinely gives me chills — the spot where Eom Heung-do secretly buried him became, some 240 years later under King Sukjong, the official royal tomb 'Jangneung.' A place one man protected at the cost of his own safety eventually got formally recognized, just a couple centuries late.

I haven't been to Yeongwol yet, but after this film I want to go. Not really for a filming-location photo, but to see the river Danjong actually looked at.

The movie set is gone, but the place where that story began — and the place where it was finally, belatedly honored — are both still there.

청령포 (강원 영월군 남면 광천리)

A "land-locked island" surrounded on three sides by the Seogang River and blocked behind by steep peaks. This is where Danjong was first exiled in June 1457 after his deposition, and it can still only be reached by ferry today. Note that the film's Cheongnyeongpo scenes were actually shot at a set in nearby Seondol Village due to the site's modern tourist development — this is not the filming location, but the real historical site where the events actually happened.

RELATED

단종어소 (청령포 내)

Danjong's reconstructed exile residence inside Cheongnyeongpo. Wax figures inside recreate his life in exile, letting visitors gauge the size and isolation of the room where the deposed 17-year-old king actually stayed.

RELATED

관음송 (청령포 내)

A 600-year-old, 30-meter pine tree whose name — combining the characters for "see" and "sound" — comes from a legend that it both saw and heard Danjong weeping. Designated a natural monument.

RELATED

엄흥도 소나무 (청령포 내)

A pine tree in front of Danjong's residence, oddly bent low as if bowing. It's nicknamed after Eom Heung-do, the loyal official who risked his life to retrieve Danjong's body after his death.

RELATED

망향탑 (청령포 내)

A stone tower Danjong is said to have built stone by stone while longing for the queen and mother he left behind in Hanyang (Seoul). Located partway up the hill behind Cheongnyeongpo.

RELATED

단묘유지비 (청령포 내)

A monument marking the old site of Danjong's exile, erected in the late Joseon period after his innocence was officially restored.

RELATED

금표비 (청령포 내)

A boundary stone erected during Danjong's exile marking a line not to be crossed — a real artifact showing that Cheongnyeongpo functioned as a near-imprisonment.

RELATED

장릉 (강원 영월군 영월읍 영흥리, 유네스코 세계유산)

Danjong's royal tomb. The spot where Eom Heung-do secretly buried him became an official royal tomb — Jangneung — some 240 years later, when Danjong was posthumously restored as king under Sukjong. It is the only Joseon royal tomb located outside the Seoul-Gyeonggi area, itself a mark of how exceptional his death was. Listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site among the Joseon Royal Tombs.

RELATED

관풍헌 (강원 영월군 영월읍 중앙로)

The government guesthouse in Yeongwol where Danjong was moved after two months, once Cheongnyeongpo faced flood risk during monsoon season. He lived here for roughly two more months before being forced to drink poison and dying. His final residence and the site of his death.

RELATED

자규루 (관풍헌 옆, 강원 영월군 영월읍)

Originally named Maejungnu, this pavilion was renamed Jagyuru after Danjong composed the poem "Jagyusa" (Song of the Cuckoo) here, comparing his own plight to a cuckoo's cry. The poem remains a defining piece of literature representing Danjong's sorrow.

RELATED

선돌 (강원 영월군 영월읍 방절리, 영월 10경)

A towering rock formation on a cliff above the Seogang River, one of Yeongwol's Ten Scenic Views. Danjong's exile road to Cheongnyeongpo is said to have followed the riverside path beneath this cliff.

FILMED HERE

선돌마을 (강원 영월군 영월읍 방절리)

After searching the whole country, the film crew found that this village's waterway and mountain terrain remarkably resembled Cheongnyeongpo. Since the real Cheongnyeongpo is too developed for tourism to allow filming, a set recreating old Cheongnyeongpo was built here, and most of the exile scenes were shot at this actual location.

소나기재 전망대 (강원 영월군 영월읍)

An overlook offering a sweeping view of Seondol Village, the Seogang River, and the Seondol rock formation together — also the best vantage point for viewing the film's Seondol Village set.

FILMED HERE

어라연 (강원 영월군 김삿갓면 거운리)

A scenic gorge on the Donggang River where jagged cliffs rise along the waterway. The scene of Danjong crossing the river by raft during his exile journey was actually filmed here.

FILMED HERE

판운리 섶다리 (강원 영월군 한반도면 판운리)

A traditional bridge made of wood and earth, a well-known feature of the Donggang River — rebuilt each winter and washed away each monsoon season. It served as a backdrop for scenes of Danjong's exile journey.

RELATED

배일치재 (강원 영월군 진입로 고갯마루)

A mountain pass where Danjong is said to have bowed in longing toward the queen and mother he left in Hanyang. A stone statue depicting him bowing, along with a monument, still stands at the summit today. Part of the 44.5km "King Danjong's Exile Road" trail.

RELATED

창절서원 (창절사, 강원 영월군 영월읍 팔괴리)

A Confucian academy that began as a shrine honoring the spirits of the Six Martyred Ministers, later expanded in 1828 to also enshrine Eom Heung-do alongside them. The representative site in Yeongwol for honoring the Six Martyred Ministers.

RELATED

금강공원 (낙화암 옛터, 강원 영월군 영월읍 봉래산)

The former site of Nakhwaam ("Falling Flowers Rock"), where Danjong's attendants are said to have thrown themselves into the river after his death. Recent "Bongnaesan landmark development" construction of a scenic footbridge and observation tower has considerably changed its original appearance.

RELATED

창덕궁 (서울 종로구 율곡로, 유네스코 세계유산)

The palace from which Danjong departed on June 22, 1457, to begin his journey into exile. His final farewell to Queen Jeongsun is said to have taken place nearby. The journey to Cheongnyeongpo took seven grueling days.

RELATED

경복궁 근정전 (서울 종로구 사직로)

The site where Sejo was formally enthroned in 1455 in a coerced abdication ceremony forced upon Danjong. A symbolic stage for the Sejo regime that had fully seized power after the Gyeyu Coup.

RELATED

사육신공원 (서울 동작구 노량진동)

The site of the tombs of the Six Martyred Ministers — Seong Sam-mun, Bak Paeng-nyeon, Ha Wi-ji, Yi Gae, Yu Seong-won, Yu Eung-bu, and the posthumously recognized Kim Mun-gi. Sanctified and developed into a park in 1978; the tombs are designated a Seoul Tangible Cultural Property.

RELATED

민절서원 (사육신공원 내)

A Confucian academy built in 1681 by King Sukjong to honor the loyalty of the Six Martyred Ministers. Sanctified together with their tombs to form today's Sayuksin Park.

RELATED

정업원구기 (서울 종로구 숭인동)

The former site of Jeongeobwon, the Buddhist convent where Queen Jeongsun lived as a nun after parting with Danjong. King Yeongjo later erected a monument here to commemorate this history.

RELATED

청룡사 (서울 종로구 숭인동)

The temple that succeeded Jeongeobwon. Danjong is said to have shared his final farewell with Queen Jeongsun at this temple's Uhwaru pavilion before departing for Yeongwol.

RELATED

동망봉 (서울 종로구 숭인동, 낙산 지봉)

The peak Queen Jeongsun is said to have climbed daily, gazing toward Yeongwol and weeping in longing for Danjong. The characters "東望峯" (Dongmangbong, "Gazing East Peak") are carved into the rock face.

RELATED

자지동천 (서울 종로구 숭인동)

A well site tied to a legend that Queen Jeongsun dyed cloth purple here to make a living. The story reflects the impoverished life she led after her husband's exile, despite once having been queen.

RELATED

사릉 (경기 남양주시 진건읍, 유네스코 세계유산)

Queen Jeongsun's royal tomb. Having lived alone for 64 years after Danjong's death, she was buried separately here rather than alongside him. Listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site among the Joseon Royal Tombs.

RELATED

금성단 (경북 영주시 순흥면)

The site where Prince Geumseong, while in exile, plotted Danjong's restoration with Sunheung magistrate Yi Bo-heum. When the plot was discovered — an incident known as the Jeongchuk Rebellion — the entire Sunheung district was abolished and many residents were punished along with him. In the 18th year of King Yeongjo's reign, the Geumseongdan altar was built to honor Prince Geumseong's righteous death.

RELATED

강화도 (인천 강화군)

The island where Prince Anpyeong was exiled and ultimately forced to end his life after the Gyeyu Coup. The site where a prince celebrated as a master of poetry, calligraphy, and painting became a victim of political purge.

RELATED

낙빈서원(육신사) (대구 달성군 하빈면 묘리)

A Confucian academy in Daegu housing "Yuksinsa," a shrine dedicated to the Six Martyred Ministers. Built as Nakbin Seowon in 1691, memorial rites for the Six Martyrs continue here to this day.

RELATED

충절사 (경북 문경시)

A shrine in Mungyeong honoring Eom Heung-do's loyalty, built in the area where he is said to have lived in hiding after leaving Yeongwol.

RELATED

동학사 (충남 공주시 반포면, 계룡산)

A temple in the foothills of Gyeryongsan Mountain where Eom Heung-do is said to have observed the traditional three-year mourning period for Danjong after leaving Yeongwol.

RELATED

성삼문 선생 유허지 (충남 홍성군 홍북읍, 노은서원)

The birthplace of Seong Sam-mun, at his maternal family's home. A shrine was built here in 1676, later expanded to also honor Bak Paeng-nyeon, Yi Gae, Yu Seong-won, Ha Wi-ji, and Yu Eung-bu, becoming known as Noeun Seowon. The Chungmunsa shrine building still stands.

RELATED

서산서원 (경북 상주시)

A Confucian academy honoring all six of the "Living Six Loyal Subjects" — Kim Si-seup, Won Ho, Yi Maeng-jeon, Jo Ryeo, Seong Dam-su, and Nam Hyo-on. Jo Ryeo was enshrined first, and the other five joined him in 1706 during King Sukjong's reign, forming the shrine as it stands today.
