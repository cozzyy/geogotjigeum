Inside This Scene

The Glory Hotel had a real-life model

덕수궁 대한문(미스터 션샤인의 실제 역사적 배경인 대한제국 정궁 정문) · Wikimedia Commons

The Story

Finish Mr. Sunshine and the Jeong-dong district keeps pulling at you.

I looked up whether the Glory Hotel, where the five leads' lives entangle, was ever a real place.

Not under that exact name, but it had a real prototype: the Sontag Hotel, Korea's first Western-style hotel, opened in 1902. It served as a social hub for foreign envoys and merchants; the building itself no longer stands, but a marker remains on the campus of Ewha Girls' High School.

The scene where the Eulsa Treaty is signed isn't fiction either. Jungmyeongjeon, where that treaty was actually signed in 1905, still stands in Seoul's Jeong-dong today, complete with a permanent exhibition inside. Standing there in person, the weight of that scene from the show lands a little differently.

근정전, 경복궁 — 미스터 션샤인 시대 배경 궁궐 · Wikimedia Commons

On the other hand, the Musinhoe's base in Japan — the pro-Japanese armed organization in the drama — isn't a real place. In keeping with this site's principle of not assigning coordinates to unconfirmed locations, this entry isn't shown on the map at all.

Apart from walking Seoul's Jeong-dong, there's also a place where the entire 1900s Hanseong setting was recreated at full scale. Near Tapjeong Lake in Nonsan, South Chungcheong, the production built five modern-style buildings, 19 tile-roofed houses, 4 thatched houses, and 9 Japanese-style houses — a filming set that's still open to visitors today.

Rewatching this drama, you feel that the label 'historical romance' doesn't fully capture it. Eugene Choi, born the son of a slave, loses his parents right after the Sinmiyangyo and flees to America, only to return to Korea as a US Marine officer; Ae-shin, born to become the mistress of a noble household, picks up a gun instead. The very fact that these two meet is evidence that Joseon's caste system is already collapsing.

Each of the five leads holds onto something different. Eugene is torn between revenge and belonging; Ae-shin between class and conviction; Gu Dong-mae, a Musinhoe veteran, between survival and loyalty; Kim Hui-seong, the son of a noble family, between family and conscience; Kudo Hina, a Japanese intelligence agent, between information and human warmth. The tension created by these five axes is exactly why this drama transcends a simple love story.

From the moment news of the Eulsa Treaty's signing spreads, the choices these five people make become irreversible. What gives this drama real power is that even as it foregrounds romance, it never treats the weight of this history lightly.

경회루, 경복궁 — 미스터 션샤인 시대 배경 궁궐 · Wikimedia Commons

When Gojong's forced abdication and the disbandment of the army spark the nationwide Jeongmi righteous-army uprising, the drama doesn't shy away from showing Ae-shin and the others plunging headfirst into armed resistance as unnamed fighters.

Exactly what choices each of these five people makes, and where each of them ends up, I won't reveal all of that here. But I do want to say upfront what really sits beneath this show's title: it's a love story, and also a belated tribute to those who fell without ever being named.

So if you finish this drama remembering only how things end for Eugene and Ae-shin, you've only seen half of it. This story is only complete once you also take in the unnamed righteous-army fighters who fell, and the drama's own effort to remember them.

Actually walking through Seoul's Jeong-dong district makes clear just how concretely this drama is grounded in real history. I'd recommend starting at Deoksugung's Daehanmun gate and walking through Jungmyeongjeon, the former Russian Legation site, and Jeongdong Church.

And if you also walk through the full-scale 1900s Hanseong set actually built at Tapjeong Lake in Nonsan, you'll likely come away understanding firsthand why this drama has stayed with so many people.

한성 (현재 서울)

The capital of the Korean Empire, a city in upheaval where foreign legations, palaces, trolleys, and modern streets mixed with traditional markets. This is the real stage where the five leads' lives intersect. This site aims to overlay today's Seoul street names with the legations, palaces, trolleys, and streets of 1900s Hanseong — so you can see what a street you're walking on today once was.

FILMED HERE

광화문 (경복궁 정문, 서울 종로구 사직로 161)

The main gate of Gyeongbokgung Palace, a symbolic gateway to the Korean Empire's royal authority. It still stands in the same spot in the heart of downtown Seoul today, a symbolic face of the royal power behind the story's events. It suffered repeated damage and restoration since the late Joseon era; today's structure was restored in 2010.

FILMED HERE

근정전 (경복궁 정전)

Gyeongbokgung's main throne hall, the most formal space where the king received court officials. It continued to function as a symbolic space for royal ceremony during the Korean Empire era. This is the real heart of the royal power the drama depicts.

FILMED HERE

경회루 (경복궁 내 누각)

A pavilion built over a pond, used for state banquets and receiving important guests. A symbolic space where traditional architecture met modern diplomacy — the kind of setting the Korean Empire court might have used to receive foreign envoys.

FILMED HERE

건청궁 (경복궁 내, 을미사변 현장)

Where King Gojong and Empress Myeongseong resided, and the actual site of the 1895 Eulmi Incident in which she was assassinated by Japanese agents. The most direct historical backdrop to the tragic era the drama's characters live through — a site of real state violence that calls for factual, respectful treatment rather than sensationalism.

FILMED HERE

경운궁 (현 덕수궁, 서울 중구 세종대로 99)

The Korean Empire's main palace where Emperor Gojong actually conducted state affairs, home to several modern-era buildings including Jungmyeongjeon, where the 1905 Eulsa Treaty was signed. The real stage for many of the drama's royal-court storylines, and a unique space where traditional wooden architecture and Western stone buildings coexist within one palace.

FILMED HERE

대한문 (경운궁·덕수궁 정문)

The main gate of Gyeongungung, sharing its name with the Korean Empire (Daehan) itself — a symbolic entrance. A guard-changing ceremony is still held here daily, making it one of downtown Seoul's signature sights.

FILMED HERE

중화전 (덕수궁 정전)

Gyeongungung's main throne hall, where Gojong received his officials as Emperor of the Korean Empire. Built shortly after the Korean Empire was proclaimed, it symbolizes the era's aspiration toward an independent modern nation-state.

FILMED HERE

함녕전 (덕수궁 내, 고종의 침전)

Gojong's actual sleeping quarters, where he later passed away. Unlike the formal throne hall, this was where the emperor's private daily life took place — the real space closest to the "emperor's day" the drama's characters imagine.

FILMED HERE

즉조당 (덕수궁 내)

One of Deoksugung's smaller audience halls, known as the site where Gojong was enthroned as Emperor of the Korean Empire — one of the symbolic starting points of the era itself.

FILMED HERE

석어당 (덕수궁 내, 2층 목조 건물)

A plain, unpainted two-story wooden building within Deoksugung, standing in contrast to the palace's more ornate halls — a trace of restraint amid an era of upheaval.

FILMED HERE

정관헌 (덕수궁 내, 서양식 정자)

A Western-style pavilion where Gojong drank coffee and received diplomatic envoys, reportedly involving Russian architect Afanasy Sabatin in its design. A symbolic space showing how the Korean Empire brought Western culture into the palace itself — a real, royal-court counterpart to the "modern coffee culture" several characters enjoy in the drama.

준명당 (덕수궁 내)

A hall connected to Jeukjodang by a corridor, also used by Gojong to receive foreign envoys — one of several palace spaces where modern diplomacy actually took place.

광명문 (덕수궁 내 문)

Hamnyeongjeon's south gate, now used to display artifacts like the Jagyeongnu water clock. A reminder that every gate in the palace once served a real functional purpose.

돈덕전 (덕수궁 내, 2023년 복원)

A Western-style building where the Korean Empire received foreign envoys and held state banquets — also the site of Emperor Sunjong's enthronement ceremony. Long lost, it was historically reconstructed and reopened in 2023. A space condensing the Empire's effort to present itself to the world as a modern power.

FILMED HERE

석조전 (덕수궁 내, 서양식 석조 건물)

The Korean Empire's first Western-style stone palace building, notable for its Greek-temple-like colonnade. It now houses a history museum recreating the Western-style furniture and daily life of the imperial court at the time.

덕수궁 돌담길

A scenic walking path along Deoksugung's western wall, flowing naturally into Jeong-dong's cluster of modern-era buildings. One of Seoul's best-loved walking routes today, and a relaxed way to feel the era's atmosphere.

FILMED HERE

중명전 (서울 중구 정동)

The Western-style building where the 1905 Eulsa Treaty was actually signed — the decisive moment the Korean Empire lost its diplomatic sovereignty. One of the heaviest historical sites tied to this drama, showing exactly where the era's catastrophe, toward which the characters' choices are all heading, actually took place.

FILMED HERE

구 러시아공사관터 (서울 중구 정동, 탑만 현존)

The site where Gojong, fearing for his life after the Eulmi Incident, fled with the crown prince in 1896 — the Agwan Pacheon (Royal Refuge at the Russian Legation). Most of the building was destroyed during the Korean War; only the tower remains. A site that testifies to the real depth of the crisis facing the royal court the drama depicts.

FILMED HERE

손탁호텔터 (현 이화여자고등학교 내)

Korea's first Western-style hotel, opened in 1902 — arguably the real historical prototype for the drama's fictional Glory Hotel. It served as a social hub for foreign envoys and merchants; in 1917 Ewha Hakdang bought it for use as a dormitory. The building no longer stands; only a commemorative marker remains on the Ewha Girls' High School campus.

FILMED HERE

정동제일교회 (서울 중구 정동)

Korea's first Western-style Protestant (Methodist) chapel, completed in 1897, still an active church holding real services today. One of the actual channels through which modern religion and education entered Joseon via this Jeong-dong district.

FILMED HERE

배재학당역사박물관 (서울 중구 정동)

The former east wing of Paichai Hakdang, Korea's first Western-style modern school, founded in 1885 — now a history museum. One of the real gateways through which American culture and modern education reached Joseon's young people in this era.

FILMED HERE

이화학당 심슨기념관 (이화여자고등학교 내)

One of the original buildings of Ewha Hakdang, Korea's first modern educational institution for women, founded in 1886 — now home to the Ewha Museum. A real site of the era's radical shift toward modern education for women.

FILMED HERE

구 미국공사관 (현 주한미국대사관저·하비브하우스)

In continuous use by American diplomats since 1883, one of Seoul's oldest Western-style diplomatic compounds. The real historical basis for the US legation Eugene Choi serves in the drama, and still an active diplomatic residence (Habib House) today.

FILMED HERE

구 영국공사관 (현 주한영국대사관)

Completed in 1890, one of the oldest Western-style diplomatic buildings in Seoul, still in active use as the British Embassy today. Shows the diplomatic landscape of an era when the great powers built their legations side by side in the Jeong-dong district.

HANDS-ON

서울시립미술관 서소문본관 (옛 대법원 건물)

An art museum housed in the remodeled former Gyeongseong Court/Supreme Court building from 1928, standing within Jeong-dong's cluster of modern-era architecture. Though built just after the drama's era, it continues the district's role as a center of modern law and administration.

HANDS-ON

구세군중앙회관 (서울 중구 덕수궁길)

A building marking the arrival of modern religious and social-welfare organizations in Seoul, part of the cluster of modern-era architecture near Deoksugung. Connects to the era's broader trend of Western religious and charitable organizations establishing themselves in Joseon.

HANDS-ON

정동극장 (서울 중구 정동)

A performance venue built in the spirit of Wongaksa, Korea's first modern theater, continuing Jeong-dong's identity as a district of modern culture and arts. A place where today's performances let you feel the atmosphere of a street once flooded with new, modern culture.

FILMED HERE

운현궁 (서울 종로구 삼일대로30길 45)

The real private residence of Heungseon Daewongun, Gojong's father, and also Gojong's own birthplace and childhood home. A royal private residence that also functioned as a real center of political power, showing the roots of the royal authority the drama depicts.

FILMED HERE

종로 (서울 종로구)

Hanseong's central commercial street since the Joseon era, home to the licensed royal merchants (yugujeon). The real prototype for the streets of Hanseong the drama depicts, where traditional commerce and modern imports mixed in the early years of modernization.

FILMED HERE

보신각 (서울 종로구 종로2가)

A belfry that once signaled the hours, structuring daily life in Hanseong since the Joseon era, now well known for its New Year's Eve bell-ringing ceremony. Shows that bell-ringing, like the drama's righteous-army character disguised as a bell keeper, was a real, official civic duty.

FILMED HERE

탑골공원 (서울 종로구 종로 99)

Korea's first Western-style modern park, established in 1897. A site showing how the very concept of a public urban park was itself a new, modern idea introduced during this era.

HANDS-ON

한성부 관아터 (현 서울시청 인근)

The site of Hanseongbu, the administrative office governing Hanseong during the Joseon and Korean Empire eras, near today's Seoul City Hall. Shows how today's Seoul city administration traces its roots back to this era's Hanseongbu.

FILMED HERE

숭례문 (남대문, 서울 중구 세종대로 40)

The southern main gate of Hanyang's fortress walls, and the real site of the 1907 Battle of Namdaemun — fought when Korean soldiers revolted after Japan ordered the disbandment of the Korean Empire's army. The real historical site of the kind of armed resistance the drama depicts.

FILMED HERE

흥인지문 (동대문, 서울 종로구 종로 288)

The eastern main gate of Hanyang's fortress walls, a key gateway into and out of Hanseong along with Namdaemun. A symbolic marker of the city's boundary as it expanded inside and outside the walls during modernization.

FILMED HERE

한성전기회사터 (옛 동대문 발전소 부지 일대)

The real site of the Hanseong Electric Company, which ran the Korean Empire's trolley, electric lighting, and telephone services, with a power plant and trolley depot located just inside Dongdaemun. The real historical prototype for the bell-towered Hanseong Electric Company building recreated in the drama.

FILMED HERE

명동성당 (서울 중구 명동길 74)

Korea's first Gothic-style cathedral, completed in 1898 — a symbolic example of modern Western religious architecture rising in the heart of Hanseong. A visible sign of how Western culture, through religion and architecture, was entering the city during the drama's era.

FILMED HERE

경희궁 (서울 종로구 새문안로 45)

A secondary Joseon-era palace that was heavily damaged during the Japanese colonial period, with only part of it restored today. One of several palaces held by the Korean Empire court, showing how the palace spaces themselves were reduced and damaged as the era changed.

FILMED HERE

구 서울역사 (당시 남대문정거장, 서울 용산구)

The gateway of modern rail transport as the Gyeongin and Gyeongbu lines were built, known during the drama's era as "Namdaemun Station." The real gateway through which people, goods, and foreign influence flowed into Hanseong by rail.

환구단 (원구단, 현 웨스틴조선호텔 자리)

The real site where Gojong held his imperial enthronement ceremony and proclaimed the Korean Empire in 1897. Most of the complex is gone today; only the auxiliary Hwanggungu structure remains beside the hotel that now occupies the site. The symbolic birthplace of the very name "Korean Empire" that frames the drama.

HANDS-ON

서소문역사공원 (서울 중구 칠패로)

The site of the former execution grounds just outside Seosomun (Soeuimun) gate, a place layered with painful chapters of modern Korean history. Even before the era of sovereignty loss the drama depicts, this ground had already witnessed sacrifices across multiple eras.

HANDS-ON

한양도성 (낙산 구간)

The Joseon-era fortress wall encircling Hanseong, which continued to define the city's boundary well into the modern era. One of the few places today where you can walk the wall trail and still trace the outline of old Hanseong.

HANDS-ON

북촌한옥마을 (서울 종로구)

A dense cluster of traditional hanok houses on the hillside between Gyeongbokgung and Changdeokgung, once home to the yangban aristocracy of the Joseon and Korean Empire eras. The most vivid place today to feel the kind of residential setting a noble young woman like Go Ae-shin might really have lived in.

HANDS-ON

남산골한옥마을 (서울 중구 퇴계로34길 28)

A traditional village created by relocating and restoring five Joseon-era hanok houses, designed to let visitors experience daily life of the period. A place where you can compare, side by side, the kinds of homes characters of different classes in the drama might have lived in.

FILMED HERE

서대문형무소역사관 (서울 서대문구 통일로 251)

A prison built in 1908, the real site where anti-Japanese independence activists were actually imprisoned and tortured starting just after the drama's era. A symbolic space showing how real the dangers facing the drama's righteous-army fighters truly were — a history of state violence that calls for a respectful visit.

FILMED HERE

독립문 (서울 서대문구)

Built in 1897 on the site of Yeongeunmun, the gate once used to welcome Qing envoys, a symbolic expression in architecture of the era's aspiration for independence. Even before the drama's timeframe, Joseon was already making various attempts to defend its sovereignty.

FILMED HERE

성균관 (서울 종로구 성균관로 25-2)

Joseon's highest national educational institution, still a center of Confucian learning through the Korean Empire era. The real setting for the kind of traditional education noble-family children like Kim Hui-seong or Go Ae-shin would plausibly have received.

FILMED HERE

종묘 (서울 종로구 종로 157)

The royal ancestral shrine of the Joseon and Korean Empire monarchy, a UNESCO World Heritage site. Shows that the royal court the drama depicts was not just a political power but also the bearer of a religious legitimacy tied to ancestral rites.

FILMED HERE

사직단 (서울 종로구 사직로 89)

A state altar for rites to the gods of soil and grain, which together with Jongmyo formed the phrase "jongmyo-sajik" — a term symbolizing the nation itself. The very concept of "the nation" that the Korean Empire fought to protect is condensed into this altar.

FILMED HERE

남산 왜성대 (옛 일본공사관터, 현 서울애니메이션센터 일대)

The Namsan hillside where Japan's legation actually stood during the Korean Empire era, later becoming the real base for the Residency-General and then the Governor-General's office. The real geographical backdrop for the drama's events involving the Japanese legation.

HANDS-ON

소공동 (남별궁터, 서울 중구)

The site of Nambyeolgung, where Joseon once received Chinese envoys — later the very ground on which the Hwangudan altar was built. The shift from China-centered diplomacy to Korean Empire self-determination is layered into this single site.

HANDS-ON

을지로 (옛 황금정)

A street where commercial and financial facilities began clustering after modernization, then known as Hwanggeumjeong. Alongside Jongno's traditional royal-merchant district, this was another axis where new capital and commerce took root.

HANDS-ON

상동교회 (서울 중구 소공로)

A modern-era church known to have been frequented by figures involved in the patriotic enlightenment movement, showing how religious spaces sometimes doubled as bases for national salvation and reform activities.

육조거리 (현 세종대로, 정부서울청사 앞)

The street once lined with the Six Ministries and other key government offices, corresponding to today's Sejong-daero and Gwanghwamun Plaza. Still the central axis of national administration during the Korean Empire era, and still home to the Government Complex Seoul today.

구 경성부청사터 (현 서울도서관)

Today's Seoul Metropolitan Library building was actually built in 1926, a bit after the drama's era, but the same site has continuously served as a center of city administration ever since — a prime example of the site's "Hanseong-meets-today's-Seoul" overlay concept.

HANDS-ON

서울광장 (서울시청 앞)

This area was already a symbolic gathering place during the Joseon and Korean Empire eras, and today Seoul Plaza in front of City Hall still hosts large civic events. The role of a space "where people gather to share their voice" has continued across eras.

제물포로 (경인가도, 한성-제물포 연결로)

The real old road connecting Hanseong to the open port of Jemulpo (today's Incheon), Korea's first modern paved road. The road the drama's characters would plausibly have traveled between Seoul and Incheon — the actual channel through which modern goods flowed from the port into Hanseong.

HANDS-ON

정동 근대역사거리 (통합 도보 구간)

Deoksugung, Jungmyeongjeon, the Russian Legation site, the Sontag Hotel site, Jeongdong Church, Paichai Hakdang, and the US and UK legations all sit within about a 1km radius — a condensed walk through the Korean Empire's modern history. The single densest walking route for experiencing the "diplomacy and modernization of Hanseong" the drama depicts.

인천 개항장 거리 (인천 중구 신포로)

A real historic district where foreign concessions were established after the 1883 port opening, still dense with modern-era buildings today. The real prototype for the port where Eugene Choi first sets foot back on Korean soil, and the gateway through which Ae-shin dreams of reaching America.

인천 舊 일본 제1은행지점 (현 인천개항박물관)

A Western-style bank building from 1899, showing how foreign capital actually penetrated Joseon in the early port-opening era. Now the Incheon Open Port Museum, exhibiting the port district's period scenery.

대불호텔 전시관 (인천 중구)

Known as Korea's first Western-style hotel, opened in 1888 — an even earlier prototype of modern lodging than the Sontag Hotel. A reconstructed exhibit of the real space where foreigners passing through the port district actually stayed.

인천항 세관부두터 (인천 중구 항동)

A pier where Joseon's customs administration actually took place after the port opening, the real gateway through which foreign merchant ships and warships passed. Connects historically to the backdrop for Eugene's landing in Korea as a US Marine.

자유공원 (인천 중구, 옛 각국공원)

Originally the "International Park" jointly established by several nations at the time of the port opening, one of the earliest examples of Western-style park culture arriving in Joseon. From this hill overlooking the sea off Incheon, you can survey the very spot where the tide of modernity first arrived.

인천 차이나타운

A street that began as the Qing (Chinese) concession after the port opening, still showing today the multinational character of a port district where Western, Japanese, and Chinese concessions once existed side by side. A real trace of an era when multiple nations' orders overlapped within a single city.

A street clustered with modern-era financial buildings like the former Incheon branches of the 18th and 58th Japanese Banks, showing how the open port was a real arena of competing international capital.

A strategic point where foreign fleets anchored after the port opening, close to the waters where the Battle of Chemulpo Bay was fought just before the outbreak of the Russo-Japanese War. Connects to the real waters where the international tensions the drama depicts actually played out.

A stone arch gate opened by Japan in 1908 to connect the open-port district with its hinterland, an example of modern engineering literally reshaping the city's terrain. Built just after the drama's era, but part of the continuous modernization of the open-port district.

강화 초지진 (인천 강화군 길상면)

The real site of the 1871 Battle of Ganghwa (Sinmiyangyo), where American and Korean forces clashed. Joseon suffered heavy losses in the fierce fighting, and its brutality helped shape long-standing anti-foreign sentiment. One of the actual points of origin for the kind of historical trauma the generation of Eugene Choi's parents would have lived through.

FILMED HERE

강화 광성보 (인천 강화군 불은면)

The site of the fiercest fighting in the Battle of Ganghwa, where Korean defenders under General Eo Jae-yeon fought to near-annihilation rather than surrender. The real history of those who risked their lives to defend the nation connects directly to the spiritual roots of the righteous army the drama portrays.

FILMED HERE

강화 연무당 옛터 (인천 강화군 강화읍)

The real site where the 1876 Treaty of Ganghwa (Japan-Korea Treaty of Amity) was signed — the historical point where Joseon first entered the modern international treaty system. Arguably the true starting point of the port-opening and modernization the drama depicts.

FILMED HERE

강화 덕진진 (인천 강화군 불은면)

A key defensive fort along the Ganghwa Strait that engaged foreign fleets in both the 1866 French campaign and the 1871 American campaign. A site layered with real traces of Joseon repeatedly trying to defend itself against foreign military force.

HANDS-ON

전등사 (인천 강화군 길상면)

A historic temple dating back to the Three Kingdoms period, also a site of fierce fighting against French forces during the 1866 campaign. A place of long religious history that was, at the same time, a site of national crisis.

HANDS-ON

강화 고인돌 유적 (인천 강화군 하점면)

A UNESCO World Heritage site showing Ganghwa's long history of habitation since prehistoric times. Long before it became a modern battleground, Ganghwa was already land holding thousands of years of history.

FILMED HERE

논산 탑정호 미스터션샤인 촬영세트 (충남 논산시 상월면 지경리)

A full-scale, purpose-built 1900s Hanseong recreation set constructed near Tapjeong Lake for the production, including 5 modern-style buildings, 19 traditional tile-roofed houses, 4 thatched houses, and 9 Japanese-style houses. A kind of "production origin point" condensing the drama's entire backdrop into one site, still open to visitors today.

FILMED HERE

고사홍 대감댁 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A tile-roofed house set built as the residence of Go Sa-hong, Ae-shin's grandfather and head of a noble family. A space of dignity and formality befitting a distinguished Joseon aristocratic household, visually conveying the weight of status Ae-shin carries as someone raised within it.

FILMED HERE

저잣거리 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A set recreating a traditional market street lined with shops and stalls, the backdrop for everyday commoner life and covert meetings and exchanges in the drama. A three-dimensional recreation of the pre-modernization commoner economy of Hanseong.

FILMED HERE

글로리호텔 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A modern-style building set recreating the Glory Hotel, the drama's symbolic central space where the five leads' lives entangle. Inspired by the real Sontag Hotel, but newly created as a space within the drama's own fictional world.

FILMED HERE

한성전기회사 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내, 종탑 건물)

A modern-style building set with a bell tower, recreated based on the real Hanseong Electric Company. Connected to the trolley-track set, visually recreating how modern technology actually transformed the streets.

FILMED HERE

전차 선로 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A track set recreating Hanseong's first trolley line, used in the drama as a symbolic device showing how modern technology transformed the streetscape itself. Based on the real historical fact that Hanseong Electric Company opened a trolley line in 1899.

FILMED HERE

붉은 아치다리 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A red-brick arch bridge set symbolizing modern civil engineering, visually contrasting the arrival of Western-style structures against traditional wooden architecture.

FILMED HERE

일본식 가옥 구역 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내, 9채)

A zone of nine Japanese-style houses used as the backdrop for characters tied to the Musinhoe and other pro-Japanese collaborators and Japanese military figures. Visually distinguishes the living spaces of characters like Lee Hyeong-tae and the Japanese military figures.

FILMED HERE

초가 구역 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내, 4채)

A zone of four thatched houses recreating the daily life of commoners and servants. Concretely shows the kind of living space characters like the Ham-an housekeeper or the manservant would plausibly have inhabited.

FILMED HERE

사진관 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A set recreating a photo studio, a piece of modern Western culture — the backdrop for scenes where characters have portrait photographs taken. Shows the era in which photography, a new recording medium, arrived in Joseon.

FILMED HERE

해관(세관) 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A set recreating open-port customs administration, visualizing the modern administrative apparatus that controlled goods and people entering and leaving Joseon.

FILMED HERE

양의원 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A clinic set reflecting the era when Western medicine arrived, showing how modern medical care gradually became part of Korean daily life.

FILMED HERE

전보국 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A set recreating a real telegraph office, showing the era's shift as information began moving far faster than before. Used in the drama as the backdrop for urgent news passing back and forth.

FILMED HERE

서양식 예배당 세트 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 내)

A set inspired by real modern-era chapels like Jeongdong First Church, the backdrop for scenes where characters encounter the new, modern religion.

HANDS-ON

탑정호 출렁다리 전망 (논산 션샤인 스튜디오 인근)

One of Korea's longest suspension bridges, spanning Tapjeong Lake near the filming set — a nearby attraction letting visitors enjoy Nonsan's natural scenery alongside the set tour.

HANDS-ON

션샤인 스튜디오 방문자센터

A visitor center offering set-tour information and drama-related exhibits, including behind-the-scenes footage and props.

HANDS-ON

대전 근대 철도 역사거리 (대전 동구 소제동)

A modern railway-worker housing district formed alongside the opening of the Gyeongbu rail line, an example of a railway literally creating a new city. Formed just after the drama's era, but continuing the trend of railways beginning to reshape Joseon's geography from this period.

HANDS-ON

대전 뿌리공원·족보박물관 인근 전통마을

A space exhibiting traditional clan culture and genealogical records, helping visitors understand the real weight that noble-family consciousness — like that of the Go or Kim families in the drama — carried in Joseon society.

HANDS-ON

대전 도산서원 (대전 동구)

A Confucian academy (seowon) from the Joseon era, offering — alongside Sungkyunkwan — a regional microcosm of the traditional education a yangban son like Kim Hui-seong would plausibly have received.

HANDS-ON

대전 한국철도문화관

A museum tracing the history of Korea's modern railways including the Gyeongbu line, helping visitors understand the full picture of the modern transport network that forms the drama's backdrop.

HANDS-ON

대전 엑스포과학공원 (근대-현대 연결 상징 공간)

A space symbolizing how the modern-technology introduction of that era eventually led to today's Korean scientific and technological development — a chance to contrast the "shock of new civilization" the drama depicts with how it unfolded a century later.

FILMED HERE

샌프란시스코 항 (미국, 실제 이민 관문)

The real port where Korean immigrants and students first set foot on American soil in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The real historical backdrop for young Eugene Choi's journey to and settlement in America, and also the actual destination of the "trip to America" Ae-shin dreams of.

HANDS-ON

미주 한인 이민사 박물관 (미국 하와이 등)

A museum documenting early Korean-American immigration history, including the sugar-plantation immigration to Hawaii beginning in 1903 — providing historical context for the real immigrant community that a character like Eugene Choi, "a Korean raised in America," represents.

HANDS-ON

미국 해군·해병 관련 역사 전시관 (미 동부 등)

An exhibition space providing the real historical context of the US Marine Corps Eugene Choi serves in, helping visitors understand how late-19th-century American overseas deployment and diplomatic policy became entangled with Joseon.

HANDS-ON

요코하마 항 (일본, 근대 개항장)

A leading modern open port in Japan, connected to the backdrop of activity for pro-Japanese and Japan-affiliated forces like the Musinhoe in the drama. A real port showing the route by which East Asian modernization passed through Japan on its way to Joseon.

THEMED

무신회 배경지 (일본 내, 정확한 실제 좌표 비공개)

A location in Japan established as the backdrop for the Musinhoe, the pro-Japanese armed organization in the drama — a fictional setting not pointing to any specific real place. In keeping with this site's principle of not assigning arbitrary coordinates to unconfirmed locations, this entry does not display an exact position on the map. Still, the narrative meaning the Musinhoe represents — an "overseas base for pro-Japanese armed power" — is an important axis for understanding the drama.

HANDS-ON

주일본 대한제국공사관 관련 역사 공간 (일본 도쿄)

A historical backdrop related to the Korean Empire's diplomatic legation in Japan, showing one of the real axes of Korea-Japan diplomatic relations the drama depicts.

HANDS-ON

강화 용흥궁 (인천 강화군 강화읍)

The former residence of King Cheoljong before he ascended the throne, showing that Ganghwa Island also has deep ties to the royal family. A space illustrating Ganghwa's dual status as both a frontline of national defense and a root of royal lineage.

FILMED HERE

강화 갑곶돈대 (인천 강화군 강화읍)

One of the sites of fierce fighting against French forces during the 1866 campaign, one of several fortification mounds guarding the Ganghwa Strait. The Ganghwa History Museum now stands alongside it, exhibiting related artifacts.
