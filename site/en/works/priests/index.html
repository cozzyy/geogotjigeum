The Priests — Filming Locations & Real Places

That 'Myeongdong Cathedral' was actually in Daegu

See every The Priests location on the map → Top 5 · Cast · All 12 locations

Father Kim Beom-shin, convinced the comatose girl Young-shin is possessed, recruits seminarian Choi Jun-ho to help him perform an unofficial exorcism — … Real filming spots, story settings and nearby travel ideas — clearly labeled.

After The Priests, that Myeongdong Cathedral scene keeps coming back to me.

Turns out that cathedral wasn't in Seoul.

Much of the aerial footage and interior scenes were actually filmed at Gyesan Cathedral in Daegu. Because its exterior resembles Myeongdong Cathedral, the film intercuts footage from both to form a single on-screen 'Myeongdong Cathedral' — the director confirmed this directly in an interview.

The narrow alley Deacon Choi walks through is the same trick — Daegu's Dongseong-ro alley, cut together with a Myeongdong-8-gil alley in Seoul.

대구 동성로 거리 풍경 · Wikimedia Commons

Gyesan Cathedral itself isn't a set, though — it's the real thing. Built in 1902-1903, Korea's third Gothic cathedral, and mass is still held there today.

As for the story itself: it starts with Young-shin, a high schooler left comatose after a car accident. Father Kim becomes convinced this isn't just a medical condition but a case of severe possession, and starts preparing an unofficial exorcism without any formal church approval.

The seminarian he recruits, Choi Jun-ho, has his own backstory — a childhood trauma of losing his younger sister, which later ties directly into why he flees the ritual in fear and then comes back.

Before the two priests even step in, there's actually another attempt first: a traditional shamanic ritual called daesalgut. It fails, leaving the two priests alone to prepare the final exorcism.

That last night takes place at Gyesan Cathedral, standing in for 'Myeongdong Cathedral' onscreen. Knowing that this dangerous, all-night ritual was filmed inside a real, century-old cathedral changes the way the scene feels.

대구 동성로 거리 풍경(2010년) · Wikimedia Commons

The story doesn't end with the film, either. In the 2025 official sequel Dark Nuns, Kang Dong-won's Deacon Choi reappears in a cameo, confirming the two films share the same '12 Shapes' mythology.

The cast deserves its own mention too — Kim Yoon-seok as Father Kim, Kang Dong-won as Deacon Choi, and Park So-dam (who won a Baeksang Best New Actress award for this role) as Young-shin. Their dense, controlled performances carry the film all the way through.

Knowing the dark exorcism scenes were shot in a real, century-old cathedral changes the air of the film a little on a rewatch.

Just going to see Gyesan Cathedral's lit-up spires after dark is enough to actually feel the film's atmosphere in person.

I won't spell out exactly what choice Choi Jun-ho makes at the very end here — but just knowing the room where it happens wasn't a set, but a real hundred-year-old cathedral, is enough to make the film hit completely differently.

Directed by Jang Jae-hyun · Released 2015

계산성당 (대구 중구 서성로 10)

A Gothic cathedral built 1902-1903, Korea's third of its kind (Historic Site No. 290). The aerial shot of Deacon Choi entering to retrieve exorcism items, and the scene of the Monsignor watching TV, were filmed here. Because its exterior resembles Seoul's Myeongdong Cathedral, footage from both was intercut on screen as a single "Myeongdong Cathedral." Director Jang Jae-hyun confirmed the filming cooperation directly in an SBS News interview.

FILMED HERE

월배성당 (대구 달서구 월배로 131-5)

The real church where the warm flashback scene of Young-shin's off-key choir audition was filmed. Its old pagoda and zelkova-tree courtyard were used to create a color contrast with the film's otherwise dark, heavy tone.

FILMED HERE

동성로 (대구 중구, 옛 대구읍성 자리)

Daegu's largest downtown district, nicknamed "Daegu's Myeongdong." The alley near Young-shin's house and the narrow street where Deacon Choi walks in prayer were filmed here (intercut with footage from a Myeongdong-8-gil alley in Seoul). Its name derives from being the main road inside the old Daegu fortress walls.

FILMED HERE

계명대학교 대명캠퍼스 (대구 남구 명덕로 104)

The real filming location for the fictional "Seoul Catholic University Seongsin Campus" that Deacon Choi attends. Scenes of the Dean interviewing Choi and Father Kim were filmed here — a campus with older architecture frequently used for film shoots.

FILMED HERE

대구가톨릭대학교 유스티노캠퍼스 (대구 남구 앞산순환로 393)

Along with Keimyung's Daemyeong campus, this real campus was used interchangeably for the fictional seminary scenes.

THEMED

신나무골 (칠곡군 지천면, 대구 근교)

Not a film location, but a real pilgrimage site essential to understanding the roots of Daegu Catholicism. Bishop Daveluy and Fr. Choi Yang-eop secretly ministered here during the 1840s-60s persecutions; roughly 37 Catholics were martyred here in the 1868 persecution. This is the real historical root of the "hidden faith to visible cathedral" arc that Gyesan Cathedral later embodies.

HANDS-ON

앞산 일대 (대구 남구)

A mountain ringing southern downtown Daegu, at whose foot cluster many of the film's Daegu locations (Keimyung, Catholic University Justino Campus). Not itself a specific scene location, but a useful geographic anchor for combining the Daegu filming sites into one route.

HANDS-ON

반월당역 일대 (대구 중구)

The transit hub connecting Gyesan Cathedral, Dongseong-ro, and Myeongdeok Station in downtown Daegu — a practical transfer point for a filming-location pilgrimage.

HANDS-ON

이상화 고택 (대구 중구 서성로)

A real heritage site near Gyesan Cathedral, part of Daegu's official Modern Culture Alley Tour (Course 2). The former home of resistance poet Yi Sang-hwa — not a film scene, but a real landmark that rounds out the walking tour alongside the Gyesan Cathedral filming site.

HANDS-ON

서상돈 고택 (대구 중구 계산동)

The former home of Seo Sang-don, leader of the National Debt Redemption Movement. Together with Gyesan Cathedral and Yi Sang-hwa's house, it forms part of the Modern Culture Alley Tour (Course 2).

HANDS-ON

계산성당 일대 야경 포인트

Known for its especially striking illuminated Gothic spires at night. A spot fans mention as a place to genuinely feel the film's dark, religious atmosphere in person.

THEMED

후속작 《검은 수녀들》(2025) 연결 — 최부제의 이후 이야기 (실존 좌표 없음)

In the official sequel Dark Nuns (2025), made by the same production company (Zip Cinema), Kang Dong-won's Deacon Choi reappears in a cameo, continuing the "12 Shapes" mythology with a new exorcist nun. No confirmed Daegu filming locations for the sequel were found in available sources, so this site maps only the verified locations from the first film.
